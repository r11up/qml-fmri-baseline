FINDING 01 — QUANTUM FIDELITY CONCENTRATES WITH REGISTER WIDTH
==============================================================
Status:  measured, reproducible
Date:    2026-08-15; revised 2026-09-16 (see REVISION HISTORY)
Code:    src/qagta/quantum/fmri_encoder.py, src/qagta/quantum/fidelity.py
Data:    ABIDE I, CC200 / C-PAC filt_noglobal, 20 subjects
Feature mode: connectivity profiles (compress_connectivity_profiles), the
         parameterisation the graph pipeline actually consumes


CLAIM
-----
Quantum state fidelity |<psi_i|psi_j>|^2 between regional embeddings loses the
bulk of its dynamic range as the qubit register widens, until too few region
pairs remain above a usable floor to populate the graph the method builds.

At 16 qubits — the width the source manuscript specified — a median of 787 of
the 19,900 region pairs exceed a fidelity of 0.01. A k = 20 neighbourhood over
200 regions draws on at least kN/2 = 2,000 distinct pairs, so the k-NN step is
selecting neighbours it does not have. Eight qubits supplies a median of 2,336
and is the widest register that clears the budget.


MEASUREMENT
-----------
20 ABIDE subjects, 200 CC200 regions each, ring-entangled encoder (seed 0),
fidelity over all 19,900 region pairs, per-subject values then averaged:

  qubits   Hilbert dim   mean fidelity   max      pairs > 0.01   median count
  ------   -----------   -------------   ------   ------------   ------------
  4        16            0.0652          0.980    48.4%           9,575
  8        256           0.0083          0.793    12.6%           2,336
  12       4,096         0.0037          0.703     6.1%           1,108
  16       65,536        0.0027          0.675     4.4%             787

Two features of this table matter and only one was noticed originally:

  1. The MEAN collapses by a factor of 24 and crosses the 0.01 floor between
     four and eight qubits.
  2. The MAXIMUM barely moves, 0.980 -> 0.675. A thin tail of strongly
     overlapping pairs survives at every width. The matrix does not become
     uniformly orthogonal; its bulk does.

Downstream numbers from the original (temporal-mode) run, NOT re-measured under
connectivity features — treat as unverified:
  edge weights after k-NN sparsification   mean 0.0016
  node-feature MAD after propagation       0.003   (manuscript reports 0.45)


MECHANISM
---------
Measure concentration. The parcellation yields 200 regional states; a
65,536-dimensional Hilbert space has vastly more room than 200 states can
populate, so the typical pairwise overlap falls toward zero. This is the
concentration-of-measure problem in quantum kernels, the same effect that
produces barren plateaus in variational circuits [Cerezo 2021, McClean 2018,
Thanasilp 2024].

Concentration alone is not the defect. The defect is the interaction between
concentration and the fixed k of the graph construction: the graph demands a
constant number of edges per node while the supply of usable pairs falls with
width.


WHERE THIS CONTRADICTS THE SOURCE MANUSCRIPT
--------------------------------------------
Section III-C of the source manuscript treats near-orthogonality as a benefit:

  "in high-dimensional Hilbert spaces, unrelated vectors tend to be close to
   orthogonal ... Low-relevance noise connections therefore land near zero
   fidelity by default, while genuine structural relationships stand out,
   giving a naturally sparser and cleaner adjacency matrix."

The measurement shows the mechanism runs out of operating room before 16
qubits. Orthogonality is selective only while enough pairs remain
non-orthogonal to fill the neighbourhood; past eight qubits they do not, so the
claimed average degree of k ~ 25 with visible DMN/ECN block structure cannot
arise at the specified width.


IMPLICATION FOR THE PAPER
-------------------------
Publishable in its own right, as a design constraint with a measured curve and
an explicit budget. For N regions and a k-nearest-neighbour graph, the register
must be narrow enough that the pairs above the usability floor outnumber kN/2.
On these data that places the operating point at 4-8 qubits.

Recommended framing: report the curve, state the budget, and cite the
concentration literature. Do not claim high-dimensional Hilbert space as an
unqualified advantage, and do not claim the metric is destroyed — it is starved,
which is a narrower and defensible statement.


REVISION HISTORY
----------------
2026-09-16. Both the CLAIM and the MEASUREMENT table were wrong as first
written, in two independent ways.

  (a) FEATURE MODE. The original table was produced with
      compress_region_timeseries (per-region temporal features), which the
      paper later identifies as an artefact and replaces with connectivity
      profiles. The superseded temporal-mode values were:

        qubits   mean      max     pairs > 0.01
        4        0.0624    0.970   47.5%
        8        0.0039    0.662    7.4%
        12       0.0003    0.144    0.6%
        16       0.0000    0.023    0.0%

      Those came from a single subject, not a cohort.

  (b) A FALSE CLAIM. The original stated "no pair of brain regions has fidelity
      above 0.01" at 16 qubits. This was false under either feature mode, and
      self-contradicting even within the old table: a maximum of 0.023 cannot
      coexist with zero pairs above 0.01. Under temporal features the true
      share was 0.0101% (a median of 3 pairs per subject, range 1-6); under
      connectivity features it is 4.4%. The "0.0%" entry was a rounding of
      0.0101% to one decimal place, and was propagated into the manuscript and
      into the figure generator as a hardcoded constant.

The figure generator now computes this panel from data rather than restating
constants: paper-tex/sept-12-support/figure-src/make_combined_figures.py,
fig_c1_fidelity().

REPRODUCE
---------
  cd github && PYTHONPATH=src ../.venv/bin/python - <<'PY'
  import glob, torch, numpy as np
  from qagta.data.abide import compress_connectivity_profiles
  from qagta.quantum.fidelity import pairwise_fidelity
  from qagta.quantum.fmri_encoder import RingEntangledEncoder

  files = sorted(glob.glob("data/ABIDE-I/ABIDE_pcp/cpac/filt_noglobal/*.1D"))[:20]
  for nq in (4, 8, 12, 16):
      ms, xs, ct = [], [], []
      for f in files:
          feats, _ = compress_connectivity_profiles(np.loadtxt(f), nq)
          torch.manual_seed(0)
          enc = RingEntangledEncoder(nq)
          with torch.no_grad():
              _, st = enc(torch.as_tensor(feats), return_state=True)
              fid = pairwise_fidelity(st)
          off = fid[~torch.eye(fid.shape[0], dtype=bool)]
          ms.append(float(off.mean())); xs.append(float(off.max()))
          ct.append(int((off > 0.01).sum()) // 2)
      print(f"{nq:2d}  mean {np.mean(ms):.4f}  max {np.mean(xs):.3f}  "
            f"median pairs>0.01 {int(np.median(ct))}")
  PY
